# Writing SAHRA questions

SAHRA is a pass-and-play family trivia night: two teams, one host reading off a
screen, a 60-second timer, everyone shouting. A question is good when the room
reacts - a groan, a "YA3NI I KNEW THAT", an argument about who remembers it
right. A question is bad when it sounds like a school exam.

The target era is **2000s to 2010s Egypt**: what people born roughly 1990-2005
grew up on. Older classics are fine as a 600 or as a "for the parents" curveball,
but the centre of gravity is Mazzika-era, MSN-era, Helmy-era Egypt.

Pack format, file naming and build rules live in `packs/TEMPLATE.md`. Read it
before writing a pack. This file is about what goes *in* the fields.

## The voice

**The Lead is the host's personality.** Dry, teasing, a little roast-y, never
mean. It sets the scene or makes the joke so the Question can stay one breath
long. Not every question needs a Lead - use one when it adds a laugh or context.

**The Question is short and read aloud.** The host says it to a loud room. If it
needs a second breath, move the setup into the Lead.

**Talk like the family talks.** Mix Egyptian English and Franco freely: "bas",
"aywa", "ya3ni", "khalas", "3ala fekra". A whole pack can be in Franco when the
topic is Egyptian life (see `07-kalam.md`, `08-e3lanat.md`). Numbers in Franco:
2 = ء, 3 = ع, 5 = خ, 7 = ح.

**Bold is a cue for the host's voice**, not decoration: the word to lean on, the
twist, the "you will get this wrong" part. *Italics* are for titles of films,
songs, series, ads.

**Roast the room, not people.** Tease the players ("Let's see who's representing
the older side of the room"), the situation ("the most dangerous activity known
to Egyptians: **finish paperwork**"), or the subject ("Very optimistic of him").
No jokes about religion, politics, appearance, or real people's private lives.

### Flat examples (never write like this)

```
**Question:** What is the word for one instalment of a series?
**Question:** How many strings does an oud usually have?
**Question:** What is the capital of Egypt?        <- and at 400 points
**Question:** Who traditionally holds the rings before the ceremony?   <- not even Egyptian
```

They are textbook questions: no scene, no memory, no joke, nothing to argue
about. If a question could appear in any country's quiz, it is not a SAHRA
question. Fix it by anchoring it to a specific Egyptian moment, show, ad, film,
song, match, product or family ritual.

## The era: where to dig

Mine specific moments, not general knowledge. Good territory:

- **Films:** the 2000s comedy wave - Mohamed Henedy, Mohamed Saad (*El Limby*),
  Ahmed Helmy (*Mido Mashakel*, *Zaki Chan*, *Keda Reda*, *Asal Eswed*), Hany
  Ramzy, Ahmed Mekky, the Hesham Maged / Shiko / Fahmy trio. Quotes everyone
  still uses, plot twists, who played whom.
- **Ramadan TV:** series, Fawazeer, prank shows (Ramez), *El Kabeer Awi*,
  sitcoms, and above all **the ads** (Vodafone, Mobinil/Orange, Etisalat, WE,
  Banque Misr, Pepsi with Amr Diab, Panda cheese). Ad jingles and slogans are
  gold.
- **Music:** music-channel era (Mazzika, Melody, Rotana), Amr Diab, Tamer Hosny,
  Hamaki, Ruby, Hesham Abbas, Sherine, Cairokee, the rise of Mahraganat. Song
  that opened a film, who featured on what, which clip had what.
- **Kids' TV:** Spacetoon, Bakkar, Captain Majid, dubbed anime, Disney in
  Egyptian dialect, Ramadan cartoons.
- **Tech and internet:** Nokia phones and ringtones, Bluetooth file sharing,
  MSN Messenger, early Facebook Egypt, Yahoo, cyber cafés, the first viral
  Egyptian YouTube moments, *El Bernameg*.
- **Football:** the 2006-2008-2010 AFCON run, Aboutrika, Hassan Shehata, El
  Hadary, Ahly-Zamalek moments, Salah's rise.
- **Daily life:** school (thanaweya 3amma, private lessons, the school canteen),
  snacks and brands (Molto, Chipsy, Hohos, Big Cola), microbuses, the family
  WhatsApp group, weddings, 3eid, soboo3.

Avoid politics and the 2011 revolution as subject matter, religion as a quiz
topic, and anything that needs one specific family's inside knowledge.

## Difficulty ladder

Every pack has six tiers. Difficulty is about **how deep the memory goes**, not
how obscure the fact is.

| Tier | Feels like | Test |
|---|---|---|
| 100 | An invitation. Everyone in the room gets it. | Would a 12-year-old and a 60-year-old both know it? |
| 200 | Familiar. You lived through it. | Would most of the room get it with a few seconds of thinking? |
| 300 | You need to actually know the category. | Would a casual fan get it after a "wait wait wait"? |
| 400 | Separates fans from casuals. | Would one person in the room shout it first? |
| 500 | Proper fan knowledge. | Would the fan get it and the rest go "OHHH right"? |
| 600 | Hard, but fair. | When the answer is revealed, does the room say "AHHHH I knew that" - never "who would know that"? |

Exact years, scores and full credits belong at 500-600 at most, and only when
the thing itself is famous. A 600 is a deep cut from something *everyone* loved,
not a trivia fact about something nobody remembers.

## Answers

- Short, sayable, judgeable by the host in one glance.
- List accepted alternatives with ` / `: `Mohamed Abou Gabal / Gabaski`.
- Franco and Arabic spellings vary - the host judges intent, so give the most
  common spelling and add a variant only when it is genuinely different.
- If the question asks for two things ("Name **both**..."), say so in bold in
  the question.

## Facts: non-negotiable

A wrong answer ruins the round and the host's credibility.

1. **Verify every question** before it goes in a pack. Put the URL you checked in
   `**Source:**`. Wikipedia, elcinema.com, YouTube (for ads and clips), official
   club or FIFA/CAF pages and news archives are fine.
2. **If you can't verify it, mark it.** Write `CHECK:` and what's uncertain in
   the `###` note line (it is never shown in the game), and list every CHECK
   item in your reply so the owner can confirm or cut it:
   ```
   ### CHECK: not sure the ad was 2012 or 2013
   ```
3. **Never invent** quotes, years, ad slogans or song credits to make a joke land.
   A real but slightly less funny fact beats a funny wrong one.

## Spoilers and variants

- A question's text must never contain another question's answer in the same
  pack. The build catches this; avoid it while writing, not after.
- Questions about the same film, ad, match or person share a `**Group:**` so they
  never land on one board.
- Aim for **3-6 variants per tier**. More variants means more games before
  repeats, and lets the draw keep spoiler pairs apart.

## Workflow

1. Read `packs/TEMPLATE.md` and one or two gold packs (`01-cine.md`,
   `05-tofoola.md`, `04-akl.md`).
2. Brainstorm 2-3x more moments than you need for the category, sorted roughly
   by tier. Kill anything generic.
3. Write the questions. Lead for the laugh, Question for the read-aloud.
4. Verify facts, fill `Source`, mark `CHECK:` items.
5. Save new packs parked (`_NN-id.md`) until the owner has read them. Rewrites
   of existing packs edit the file in place.
6. Run `npm run build` and fix every error and spoiler warning for that pack.
7. Report back: the pack file, how many questions per tier, every `CHECK:` item,
   and 3-4 favourite questions so the owner can judge the tone quickly.

## Self-check before handing over

- Read every Lead + Question out loud. Over one breath? Cut.
- Could this question appear in a non-Egyptian quiz? Rewrite or cut.
- Does the 100 feel like a gift and the 600 feel fair?
- Is every fact sourced or marked `CHECK:`?
- Is there at least one laugh per tier?
